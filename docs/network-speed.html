Internet speed in your top bar

Tempo shows how fast you are downloading and uploading right now, as two small lines in the GNOME top bar: download over upload. The numbers change; the width never does, so nothing in your top bar shuffles sideways.

  • Free and open source
  • GNOME 42 to 51 on Ubuntu, Fedora, Debian, Arch and more
  • 250 KB, nothing else installed

What you get

  • Download and upload speed, updated every 3 seconds (or every 1 to 10, your choice).
  • A fixed width, measured in your own font. Other items in the top bar stay exactly where they are as the numbers change.
  • Counted once. Tempo adds up your real network cards (Wi-Fi, Ethernet, USB tethering). Traffic through VPN tunnels, Docker bridges and virtual machines is not counted a second time.
  • Short in the bar, spelled out in the menu: 892K and 1.2M in the top bar, 892 KiB/s and 1.2 MiB/s in the menu.
  • Also on the desktop: the floating system panel shows the same speeds. More about the panels.

What it costs your computer

While the screens are offNothing runs

Every few seconds Tempo reads one small file the Linux kernel keeps (/proc/net/dev) and subtracts the previous reading. No network traffic is created, and nothing is sent anywhere. How Tempo stays light.

Turn it on

  1. Install Tempo with the buttons above (the install guide has every step).
  2. Click Tempo in the top bar and open Settings.
  3. Switch on Network speed. It is off by default, like every extra.

Questions

How do I show internet speed in the top bar on Ubuntu?

Install Tempo, open its Settings and switch on Network speed. Download and upload speed appear in the top bar as two small lines. It works the same on Fedora, Debian, Arch and any other distribution with GNOME 42 to 51.

Is it bits or bytes?

Bytes per second, the same unit your browser shows for a download. 1.2M means 1.2 mebibytes per second, which is about 10 megabits per second.

Does it measure how fast my connection can go?

No. It shows the traffic that is happening right now, not a speed test. Start a big download and you will see roughly what your connection can do.

Why does it show 0K although I am online?

Because nothing is being transferred at that moment. The number moves as soon as something loads.

Does it count VPN or Docker traffic twice?

No. Only real network cards are counted, so traffic that also passes through a tunnel or a bridge is counted once.
